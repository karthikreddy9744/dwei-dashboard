# DWEI Project — Feature Dictionary

## Purpose

This document provides definitions, data sources, computation methods, interpretation guidelines, and analytical roles for all major variables used in the District Welfare Efficiency Index (DWEI) project.

The objective is to ensure that future users, researchers, policymakers, and developers can understand every variable without referring back to the notebooks.

---

# 1. IDENTIFICATION VARIABLES

---

# 2. STRUCTURAL NEED LAYER

These variables describe the socioeconomic environment within which governance operates.

They represent conditions inherited by district administrations rather than outcomes generated by them.

---

## female_literacy_pct

### Definition
Percentage of females classified as literate.

### Source
Census 2011 / SHRUG.

### Calculation
Female literates divided by total female population.

### Interpretation

Higher values indicate:

- Better educational attainment
- Greater awareness
- Better access to information

Lower values indicate:

- Educational disadvantage
- Lower human capital

### Role in DWEI

Need-layer predictor used in Ridge models.

---

## scst_pct

### Definition
Percentage of Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe population.

### Source
Census 2011 / SHRUG.

### Calculation
(SC + ST population) / Total population × 100

### Interpretation

Higher values indicate:

- Greater concentration of historically disadvantaged communities

### Role in DWEI

Need-layer predictor used to capture social vulnerability.

---

## agri_worker_pct

### Definition
Percentage of workers engaged in agriculture.

### Source
Census 2011 / SHRUG.

### Calculation
Agricultural workers divided by total workers.

### Interpretation

Higher values indicate:

- Greater dependence on agriculture
- More rural economic structure

Lower values indicate:

- Greater economic diversification

### Role in DWEI

Need-layer predictor.

---

## total_population

### Definition
Total district population.

### Source
Census 2011.

### Interpretation

Provides demographic scale.

### Role in DWEI

Reference variable only.

Not directly used in modeling.

---

## total_households

### Definition
Total number of households in the district.

### Source
Census 2011.

### Interpretation

Represents district size.

### Role in DWEI

Used in feature engineering.

---

## poverty_rate

### Definition
Estimated district poverty proportion.

### Source
SECC-derived poverty estimates.

### Interpretation

Higher values indicate greater economic deprivation.

### Role in DWEI

Structural need indicator.

---

## poverty_log

### Definition
Log-transformed poverty rate.

### Source
Derived from poverty_rate.

### Calculation

Log transformation applied to reduce skewness.

### Interpretation

Higher values indicate greater poverty burden.

### Role in DWEI

Primary poverty variable used in Ridge regression and XGBoost.

---

## night_lights_log

### Definition
Log-transformed nighttime light intensity.

### Source
VIIRS Nightlights.

### Interpretation

Higher values generally indicate:

- Greater economic activity
- Better infrastructure
- Urbanization

Lower values indicate:

- Lower economic development

### Role in DWEI

Proxy measure of economic development.

---

# 3. MGNREGS IMPLEMENTATION LAYER

These variables measure administrative and programme implementation performance.

This layer became the most influential component in SHAP analysis.

---

## wage_timeliness_pct

### Definition
Percentage of MGNREGS wages generated within the mandated payment period.

### Source
MGNREGS MIS.

### Calculation

1. Median yearly wage timeliness per district.
2. Capped at 100.
3. Averaged across 2019-20, 2020-21, and 2021-22.

### Interpretation

Higher values indicate:

- Better administrative responsiveness
- Faster wage processing
- Stronger implementation systems

Lower values indicate:

- Administrative bottlenecks
- Delayed wage payments

### Role in DWEI

Administrative efficiency indicator.

---

## avg_days_per_hh

### Definition
Average employment days provided per participating household.

### Source
MGNREGS MIS.

### Interpretation

Higher values indicate:

- Greater employment support
- Stronger programme delivery

Lower values indicate:

- Lower programme utilization

### Role in DWEI

Key implementation variable.

Major SHAP contributor.

---

## women_pct

### Definition
Percentage of women participating in MGNREGS employment.

### Source
MGNREGS MIS.

### Interpretation

Higher values indicate:

- Greater inclusion
- Better outreach
- Stronger participation

### Role in DWEI

Most influential feature in global SHAP analysis.

---

## persondays_per_hh

### Definition
MGNREGS employment intensity normalized by households.

### Source
MGNREGS MIS.

### Interpretation

Higher values indicate:

- Strong programme reach
- Greater labour engagement

### Role in DWEI

Key implementation variable.

Major SHAP contributor.

# 8. DISTRICT WELFARE EFFICIENCY INDEX

## DWEI_score

### Definition

Composite welfare efficiency score constructed from standardized residuals.

### Concept

Measures:

Observed Welfare Improvement

relative to

Expected Welfare Improvement

given district structural conditions.

### Interpretation

Positive Values

District outperformed expectations.

---

Negative Values

District underperformed expectations.

---

Near Zero

District performed approximately as expected.

### Important Note

DWEI is NOT:

- Development Index
- Poverty Index
- Prosperity Index

DWEI IS:

- Welfare Efficiency Index

# 10. MACHINE LEARNING OUTPUTS

## Predicted Tier

### Definition

Tier predicted by the XGBoost classifier.

### Purpose

Used for explainability and SHAP analysis.

---

## SHAP Values

### Definition

Feature contributions produced by SHAP.

### Interpretation

Positive SHAP:

Pushes prediction toward a tier.

Negative SHAP:

Pushes prediction away from a tier.

### Important Note

SHAP explains model behavior.

SHAP does NOT establish causality.

---

# Final Note

The DWEI framework consists of three conceptual layers:

1. Structural Need Layer
2. Administrative Performance Layer
3. Welfare Outcome Layer

The interaction of these layers produces a residual-based welfare efficiency measure that evaluates district performance relative to structural expectations rather than absolute development levels.
